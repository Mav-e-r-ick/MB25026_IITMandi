    Baseline: the level this person returns to. Inflow: glucose entering from meals, the liver or stress, which a CGM alone cannot tell apart. Both are estimated, not measured.

    How might the next 2 hours change if this patient ate now? The dashed amber line on the chart shows the scenario. It uses assumed meal-response parameters for the patient's group, not values fitted to this person, so read it as a model scenario, not a prediction or advice.

    Every warning costs clinician time. These numbers come from cross-validated replay on 45 CGMacros participants. The workload figures are illustrative and depend on the assumptions you set.

    The twin. A state-space model of sensor glucose with three hidden states: current glucose, an unexplained glucose inflow, and a slowly drifting personal baseline. A Kalman filter updates them with every reading, and handles missing readings by widening its uncertainty instead of guessing.

    The hybrid. Gradient boosting learns what the twin gets wrong and estimates the 2-hour risk from the twin's states, recent glucose patterns and the patient's profile. Intervals are 90% conformal, calibrated on held-out patients per diabetes group.

    Event definition. Any CGM reading ≥ 180 mg/dL within 120 minutes, scored only when the current reading is below 180 and at least 80% of future readings exist. 180 mg/dL is a research setting, not a treatment target.

    Honest results. The hybrid scored best on every event metric, but its gain over plain gradient boosting is not statistically significant, and plain gradient boosting transferred better to 100 external patients from Shanghai. The twin's clearest advantages: unchanged accuracy with 30% of readings missing, and a one-parameter fix for a noisy sensor (15-minute error 15.1 → 10.7 mg/dL) with no retraining.

    Limits. Trained on 45 adults in California (14 with type 2 diabetes). No meal, insulin or medication inputs. Not validated on Indian patients.

    Five-fold cross-validation by participant, 45 people, 5,427 excursion labels. MAE in mg/dL. Chance AUPRC is 0.16.
